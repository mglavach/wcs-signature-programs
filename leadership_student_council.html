What It Is

Through your Student Council, every student's ideas can be heard. It is your responsibility to represent the views of your classmates at each Student Council meeting.

Your school has a Student Council to keep the students, principal, and teachers informed of others' ideas, needs, and opinions. The Student Council also makes sure that everyone in your school cooperates in the day-to-day responsibilities of being part of a school community, enjoys special activities, and works together on specific problems.

  • All students views and ideas are presented to the Student Council through their representatives.
  • Two class representatives are elected by each homeroom to represent their grade level at Student Council meetings.
  • Officers are elected by the class representatives to lead meetings and perform special functions (see Officers).
  • Advisors are teachers who help guide the Student Council and serve as the link between the Council and the rest of the school staff (see Advisors).
  • The principal, assistant principal and school counselor are members of the Student Council; the final authority with ultimate responsibility for the school.
  • All students nominate and elect Student Council representatives and share their ideas with them.
  • Representatives hold class meetings, listen to classmates' views, and report back on what happens at Student Council meetings. They attend every Student Council meeting.
  • Officers help conduct Student Council meetings, keep records of motions, discussions, and votes, and keep track of Student Council money.
  • Advisors attend all Student Council meetings, teach rules and regulations, offer guidance, and report on Student Council actions to the school staff.
  • The principal attends meetings if needed and is the final authority on all actions of the Student Council.

Student Council members meet on Fridays during lunch. Our first meeting will be on Friday, 10/9.

Any student in good academic and behavioral standing may apply. Candidates submit an application, are approved by their gradelevel teachers, campaign for one week, and are elected by their classmates.

Who is eligible to run for Class Representative? You must...

  • Have earned all A's and B's as a final grade in core subjects on your report card last year. NO EXCEPTIONS.
  • Have earned all 1's and 2's in conduct last year and no disciplinary issues this year. NO EXCEPTIONS.
  • Have no more than one disciplinary referral (sent to principal's office) last year.
  • Be in good standing this year - class and homework assignments turned in regularly, with all A's and B's in core subjects.

Campaign Rules

Please abide by the following rules and regulations. Failure to follow these rules will result in disqualification.

1. Make a Flyer

You may hand out a flyer (no larger than 8½ x 11) to your classmates. You may not attach anything to it, and glitter is not allowed. Flyers may be handed out starting Monday, September 21.

2. Make a Poster

You may make a poster using ONLY the poster board provided by Mrs. Glavach, to be displayed inside your classroom.

3. Make a Campaign T-Shirt

You may make a campaign t-shirt to wear on Tuesday, 9/22. It must be appropriate, with no negative or derogatory slogans, and no glitter. It must be approved by your classroom teacher by Monday, September 21. Approved shirts may be worn all day Tuesday, 9/22, and during the speech on Wednesday.

4. Make a Speech

A short speech will be given on Election Day, Wednesday, September 23, stating why you would make a good representative for your class. Speeches must be submitted to your homeroom teacher for approval by Monday, September 21. New: 4th and 5th graders approved to run will be given access to a Flint lesson to help prepare their speech.

Additional Rules

  • There will be no campaigning outside the classroom.
  • Food, treats, stickers, pencils, erasers, videos, etc. are not allowed. Handing out any of these items will result in disqualification.

Writing Your Speech

Guidelines for writing your campaign speech.

1. Keep It Short and Clear

Aim for 1–2 minutes. Stay focused on the main points — don't make it too long.

2. Introduce Yourself

Start with a friendly greeting. Say your name and your grade/class. Example: "Hi, my name is [Name], and I'm in [Grade/Class]."

3. Share Why You Want to Be Class Representative

Explain what excites you about serving. Use simple, positive language. Example: "I want to help our class have fun and work together."

4. Talk About Your Strengths

Mention 2–3 qualities that would make you a good leader. Example: "I am responsible, I work hard, and I like helping others."

5. Share Ideas

Give 1–2 realistic ideas for how you could help the class or school. Do not make promises that you cannot keep. Example: "I will listen to your ideas and share them at meetings." NOT, "We will have cookies every day for lunch."

6. End with Confidence

Thank your classmates for listening. Remind them why they should vote for you. Example: "I would be honored to represent you. Please vote for me!"

Tips for Practicing Your Speech

  • Practice out loud at least 3 times.
  • Speak slowly and clearly so everyone can hear you.
  • Make eye contact with your audience.
  • Smile and show excitement!

Teacher Responsibilities

Homeroom teachers help oversee the election process in their own classroom, including:

Monitor Campaigning

Oversee campaigning and candidate speeches leading up to Election Day to keep them appropriate and on schedule.

Election Day Voting

Supervise the voting process in the classroom on Election Day.

Poster Approval

Review and approve campaign posters for appropriate content before they are displayed.

T-Shirt Approval

Review and approve campaign t-shirt designs before they are worn at school.

Officers

Elected representatives vote among themselves for three officer positions, who lead meetings and oversee Student Council responsibilities.

President

Presides over weekly meetings and represents the Council to the school community.

Vice President

Supports the President and steps in to lead meetings when needed.

Treasurer

Helps track funds and materials for Council projects and activities.

Once You Are Elected

Things to know about what you're committing to as a Student Council representative.

My Responsibilities

  • Attend all meetings if you are at school and don't have an excused absence from your homeroom teacher.
  • Be on time.
  • Take notes and keep good records so you can report back to class.
  • Come up with new ideas for projects, activities, or events.
  • Set a good example and behave well in and out of school.
  • Maintain your school work, planning ahead if necessary, to get it done.

Requirements

  • Must maintain all A's and B's on your report card. If you get a C on your report card you will have 4 weeks to bring up your grade. If you are unable, you will not be able to participate in any Student Council activities for the remainder of the quarter.
  • You are a representative of this school and should act appropriately. If you are sent to the office with a discipline referral, you will not be allowed to participate in any Student Council activities for one quarter. If there is a second referral during the school year, you will be asked to resign from your position.
  • Attendance at all meetings and events is mandatory (unless you have an excused absence from your homeroom teacher or Student Leadership sponsor).
  • You will do your best to represent WCS and Jesus!
